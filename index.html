Two engines, one map of VLA inference

vla.cpp brings a wide set of VLAs to every accelerator through ggml. vla.simd goes deep on the CPU with its own kernels and an INT8 path, down to a Raspberry Pi 5. Where one stops, the other starts.

Reach for vla.cpp when…

  • the robot has an accelerator: an NVIDIA GPU or Jetson, Apple Silicon, an Intel GPU or NPU, or a Snapdragon NPU;
  • you need a large VLA such as π0, π0.5, GR00T or OpenVLA-OFT;
  • you want one binary and one GGUF per model across all of them.

Reach for vla.simd when…

  • the robot has only a CPU, from a Raspberry Pi 5 to a desktop;
  • your policy is ACT, IMPACT, SmolVLA, Octo-Small, TurboVLA or Diffusion Policy;
  • you can trade some numerical precision for INT8 speed, or serve LeRobot's async client.

How the numbers were measured

What one number means

Latency is the wall time of one predict call in the engine's own process: image preprocessing, every encoder, all denoising steps and action decoding. It excludes the network, the simulator and the robot, and it says nothing about task success.

Actions / s is the action chunk divided by the latency: how many actions a policy can supply per second when the robot plays each chunk in full. At 30 actions/s or more it keeps up with a 30 Hz control loop.

By default the explorer shows the fastest configuration each report found for a model on a device and processor. Flash attention, lower-precision weights and INT8 can move the predicted actions, so a checkpoint's success rate at the defaults may not carry over. Tick Show every config to see the defaults as well.

vla.cpp

vla-bench on synthetic inputs, at each model's native views and input size: 3 warmups and 20 timed calls per process, 3 processes, and the process with the lowest mean is reported. Memory is the peak VRAM and host RSS, or the RSS alone on unified memory. Every device ran commit , except the Core Ultra X7 358H, at 450992c (one OpenVINO fix later).

Two rows differ: the RTX 5090 is its PR author's minimum over 5 rounds, and the Apple M5 Max comes from an earlier build. Task success is from LIBERO on an RTX 3090, 200 episodes per suite.

vla.simd

vla-simd-serve --bench with fresh random frames every call: 5 warmups and 50 timed calls (20 when a call takes more than 3 s), and the median is reported. Each model runs at its fastest measured thread count and settings, in FP32 and in INT8 (W8A8) where the CPU has AVX-VNNI or Arm dotprod. Memory is the peak process RSS.

Every device ran commit . The reports measure no accuracy; INT8 changes the numerics.
